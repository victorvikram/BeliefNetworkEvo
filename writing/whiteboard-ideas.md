# Board 1 — associative worlds

`whiteboard-associative-worlds-2026-07-23.jpeg`

## Constraints

One's set of constraints between belief splits into:

- **static / informational**
- **dynamic / causal**

*Why constraints? Because they limit the possible sets of beliefs one can hold.*

## The collective as an entity

One way to think: **the collective as an entity**. It **structures the population**.

- Also applies to identifying problems, and to any other form of collective cognition.
  What about **collective associative worlds**?
- **Non-in-brain things: they still structure what you consider to be a solution.** What
  if everyone who cares about the deficit is a Republican → only spending cuts get
  considered.
- **Collective awareness:** even if they started off as environmental, they can become
  in-brain.
- Different associative worlds help highlight associations that you might not have been
  aware of.
- Correlation + conformism can lead to genuine interaction.

## What associative worlds do

**Good:**

- Helps with **productive disagreement** — abortion as the example. You can disagree in
  beliefs, or in logic. If you disagree in logic and conclusions, the only way through is
  to rewire.
- Helps please people, since you can think of a persona and appeal to them.
  **Representation.**
- Helps you update your beliefs based on new information.

**Bad:**

- Can cause people to perceive each other as fundamentally different. **Dehumanizing.**

## Why do associative worlds matter

- **Sociality requires shared associative worlds** — they are a way to solve problems
  collectively. A shared associative world is needed to identify a common set of
  problems, and it also helps to agree on a solution.
- In fact, this is possibly **the defining property of a collective**.
- We have a desire to be understood, and sharing an associative world helps with that.
- More efficient communication.
- **Meaning-making:** helps construct an identity.
- **Helps coordination:** it helps if your beliefs are covarying in tandem with those of
  others.
- **Restricts the things you can possibly think.**

# Board 2 — hypotheses / research questions

`whiteboard-hypotheses-2026-07-23.jpeg`

Organised into five categories. `T:` is a theory/claim, `H:` the hypothesis it generates.

## Cat 1 — differences in libs & cons associative worlds (global structure)

- Social approval / consistency stronger among liberals.
- More ideas bear on any given issue for liberals, because they live in cities and in
  multicultural, **diverse environments**.
- Liberals motivated by ideals that cut across categories; conservatives more
  *practical*.
- Liberals justify things in terms of ideals and beliefs (*motivated reasoning?*).
  **(SJT)** Conservatives justify things in terms of the system — "stealing is bad
  because it's illegal" → also legality-related stuff.
- Conservatives are willing to entertain less change to the system than liberals are;
  reforms more incremental (**constraint**).
- Conservatives have more between-community diversity, liberals have less.
- Conservatives think less, so are less consistent.
- Conservatives more selfish, and vote based on personal interest.
- Liberals have a **meta-ideal** that affects everything; conservatives have a **plural
  ideal** for different spheres.

## Cat 2 — differences in libs & cons (smaller scale)

- **T:** More religious values for conservatives.
  **H:** Higher centrality between religion and politics.
- **T:** Conservatives weight "traditional virtue" more, and those affect the rest of
  beliefs more.
  **H:** Higher centrality for things related to chastity, purity, drugs, etc.
- **T:** Liberals care more about equality, conservatives about freedom.
  **H:** Equality-related things more central for libs, freedom for cons.
- **T:** Homosexuality *[added in green: and race, and abortion]* relates to many
  organising beliefs and values of libs/cons.
  **H:** Beliefs of homosexuality / race / abortion will have high centrality but
  **distinct neighbours**.
- **T:** Libs more oriented to care/harm and equality. Cons more oriented to liberty,
  sanctity, authority, loyalty/betrayal, and to fairness/cheating.
  **H:** Identify lists of variables related to these and check centrality.
- Marginal note, hard to read: *lib fairness: [equal — what you deserve]* / *cons
  fairness: [play by the rules]* — **[unclear]**.
- In green: **high centrality beliefs may be core values, or integrating challenges.**
- Cluster in the right margin, grouping the two moral vocabularies:
  freedom / tradition / religion → **SJT**; equality / care → **MFT**.

## Cat 3 — temporal evolution

- **T:** Conservatives have stronger longstanding moral foundations.
  **H:** The conservative network should evolve more slowly, especially with respect to
  moral foundations.
- **T:** Increased bifurcation between globalist / cosmopolitan / digital people versus
  local / rural.
  **H:** A conditioning that picks out these categories will show increasing divergence
  over time.
