Proposed outline for the perspective piece on belief networks.

Two worries: 
- epistemic: belief networks (in their usual form) only tell us about correlations. what use is that? In particular, there are two problems with correlations:
	- they are measured at the collective level, don’t tell us what is happening in an individual brain (ecological fallacy)
	- don’t tell us how beliefs are affecting each other – neither at the individual nor collective level
- deeper, ontological worry: belief networks seem to assume a very particular idea of what a belief is
- epistemic concerns (I suggest starting with this even though it is not the philosophical “foundation”. I think for short-attention-span empiricists, it would be good to tell them first what they can learn from belief networks)
	- works for three types of knowledge (1) passive inference, (2) upstream causation, (3) downstream causation
