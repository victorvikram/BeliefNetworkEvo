# Concept Definition and Mechanism Discussion

## Part 1: Naming the Concept

### Recommended Term: "Structural Divergence"

**One-paragraph operational definition:**

> Structural divergence is the increasing dissimilarity between the conditional
> dependency structures of ideologically defined subpopulations. It is measured
> as the Euclidean distance between group-specific partial correlation matrices
> (primary metric) and as the declining Spearman rank correlation between
> group-specific centrality hierarchies (secondary metric). Structural
> divergence is distinct from attitudinal polarization (movement to opposite
> poles on shared issues), affective polarization (increasing dislike of
> outgroups), and sorting (alignment of social identities with partisan
> identity). Two groups can hold identical position distributions on every
> issue and still exhibit structural divergence if they organize the
> relationships among those positions differently. The concept captures
> how the population-level associational architecture — which issues co-vary
> with which other issues — differs between groups and changes over time.

### Why "Structural Divergence" Over Alternatives

| Term | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|------|------|------|---------|
| **Structural divergence** | Descriptive, clean, avoids "polarization" baggage, directly maps to the measurement (diverging structures) | Somewhat generic | **Recommended** |
| Structural misalignment | Captures the centrality-disagreement finding well | Implies a baseline state of alignment that may not have existed; less intuitive | Runner-up |
| Axial divergence | Evocative — "different axes" | Too metaphorical; "axis" implies unidimensionality | Rejected |
| Centrality divergence | Precise for the specific finding | Too narrow — the Euclidean distance finding is broader than centrality | Too narrow |
| Architectural divergence | Novel, vivid | "Architecture" implies intentional design (ecological fallacy risk) | Rejected |

### Usage Rules

- Use "structural divergence" as the named concept throughout
- First use should be italicized and formally defined
- Subsequent uses can be unqualified: "structural divergence between liberal
  and conservative networks increased over the 48-year period"
- When being precise about the measurement, specify: "structural divergence,
  as measured by Euclidean distance between partial correlation matrices"
- Avoid "structural polarization" — too easily confused with existing
  polarization concepts

## Part 2: Mechanism Discussion

### Candidate Mechanisms

#### 1. Elite Cue-Giving (Zaller 1992)

**Mechanism:** Partisan elites package beliefs differently. As elite
polarization increases, the "packages" they transmit to followers become
more distinct. Followers adopt the associational structure embedded in
elite cues (e.g., if Republican elites consistently link immigration to
crime, these variables become conditionally dependent in the conservative
population network).

**Consistency with temporal trajectory:**
- Strong match. Elite polarization is well-documented since the 1970s
  (McCarty, Poole & Rosenthal 2006), and our divergence trend begins in
  the 1970s.
- The post-2008 acceleration could reflect the Tea Party and subsequent
  intensification of elite partisan messaging.

**Key prediction:** Divergence should track elite polarization measures
(DW-NOMINATE scores, platform divergence) with a lag.

#### 2. Media Sorting (Prior 2007)

**Mechanism:** Liberal and conservative media ecosystems emphasize different
issue connections. Fox News highlights crime-immigration-welfare links;
MSNBC highlights inequality-race-policing links. Differential media exposure
produces different correlation structures in the population.

**Consistency with temporal trajectory:**
- Partially consistent. Cable news expansion (1990s-2000s) and internet
  partisanship (2000s-2010s) align with the post-2004 acceleration.
- However, the divergence trend begins well before cable news (1970s),
  so media sorting cannot be the sole driver.

**Key prediction:** Divergence acceleration should correlate with media
ecosystem fragmentation measures.

#### 3. Associative Diffusion (Goldberg & Stein 2018)

**Mechanism:** Beliefs spread through association networks. When a community
is exposed to a "seed" issue (e.g., gun rights in rural conservative
communities, police accountability in urban liberal communities), related
beliefs diffuse through association. Different seed issues produce different
correlation structures.

**Consistency with temporal trajectory:**
- Directly applicable. If liberals and conservatives inhabit different
  social networks with different seed issues, associative diffusion would
  naturally produce diverging dependency structures over time.
- The slow, continuous nature of the trend (48 years) is consistent with a
  gradual diffusion process.

**Key prediction:** Variables with the largest centrality divergence should
be those with the most group-differentiated exposure patterns.

#### 4. Generational Replacement

**Mechanism:** Each cohort arrives with a different "default" belief
architecture shaped by their formative political environment. As cohorts
replace each other, the population-level network shifts. If liberal and
conservative cohorts within the same generation have increasingly different
formative experiences (different media, different communities), the
structural divergence grows with each replacement.

**Consistency with temporal trajectory:**
- Consistent with the slow, secular nature of the trend.
- However, generational replacement alone would predict a smooth, monotonic
  trend without the post-2008 acceleration.

### Framing for the Paper

**Recommended discussion paragraph:**

> We document the pattern of structural divergence but do not identify its
> causal mechanism — a task that would require panel data or experimental
> designs beyond the scope of this study. However, the temporal trajectory
> constrains the space of plausible mechanisms. The divergence trend has
> been continuous for at least 48 years (1974-2022), with a possible
> acceleration around 2008 (structural break test, p=TBD). This timeline
> rules out a purely digital explanation: the trend predates social media,
> the internet, and even cable news. It is more consistent with slower
> processes — elite polarization (Zaller 1992; McCarty et al. 2006),
> associative diffusion through increasingly segregated social networks
> (Goldberg & Stein 2018), or generational replacement of cohorts with
> different formative political environments. The post-2008 acceleration,
> if confirmed, could reflect the compounding of these slower processes
> with the amplifying effects of partisan media and social media
> ecosystems (Prior 2007). Disentangling these mechanisms is a priority
> for future work; one promising approach would be to estimate age-period-
> cohort belief networks to isolate generational from period effects.

---

### What NOT to Claim

- Do NOT claim to have identified the mechanism
- Do NOT claim the divergence is caused by cognitive differences between
  liberals and conservatives (ecological fallacy)
- Do NOT claim the divergence is caused by social media (the trend predates it)
- Do NOT use the mechanisms to make normative claims about which group is
  "right" or "wrong"
