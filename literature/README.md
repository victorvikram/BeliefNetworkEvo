# Literature

Shared papers for the two-paper package. Reference manuals such as the GSS codebook stay
in `docs/`; this folder is for work we cite and argue with.

## Present

- `DellaPosta-PluralisticCollapse-2020.pdf` — DellaPosta, Daniel. 2020. "Pluralistic
  Collapse: The 'Oil Spill' Model of Mass **Opinion** Polarization." *American Sociological
  Review* 85(3):507–536. doi:10.1177/0003122420922989. He shows beliefs cluster and that
  clustering increases, using zero-order pairwise correlations. Note he is **not** the
  closest prior work — the 2026-08-14 literature search found that is Baldassarri & Gelman
  (2008), who make the consequence claim, and Cely (2025), who makes it with our estimator.
  DellaPosta treats the structure as the *explanandum* throughout.
  *(Title corrected 2026-08-14: previously recorded as "Mass Political Polarization".)*

Added 2026-10-15 for the lead paper's spillover test. All open access; reading notes are in
`analyses/2026-10_spill-01-homosex-pilot.md` and the tracker.

- `TurnerZwinkels-DynamicConstraintNetworks-2022.pdf` — Turner-Zwinkels, Felicity M., and
  Mark J. Brandt. 2022. "Belief System Networks Can Be Used to Predict Where to Expect Dynamic
  Constraint." *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 100:104279. **The direct
  precedent**: a pooled network predicts where induced two-week change spreads.
- `FishmanDavis-ChangeWeCanBelieveIn-2022.pdf` — Fishman, Nic, and Nicholas T. Davis. 2022.
  "Change We Can Believe In." *American Journal of Political Science* 66(3). Accepted
  manuscript. **The null to answer**: centrality does not predict individual belief change.
- `Keskinturk-DynamicConstraint-2026.pdf` — Keskintürk, Turgut. 2026. "Evaluating Dynamic
  Constraint in Belief Systems." SocArXiv, doi:10.31235/osf.io/yh2fm_v2. Survey experiment;
  no induced spillover.
- `RestrepoOchoa-MeasuringMovement-2024.pdf` — Restrepo Ochoa, Nicolás, and Turgut
  Keskintürk. 2024. "Measuring Movement in Cultural Landscapes." *Poetics*,
  doi:10.1016/j.poetic.2024.101965. Accepted manuscript; check page numbers against the
  published version.
- `Kiley-PredictablyUnpredictable-Dissertation-2021.pdf` — Kiley, Kevin. 2021. Duke PhD
  dissertation; the chapter "Predictably Unpredictable" is pp. 119–176. The 2023 manuscript of
  that title is not public.
- `Guo-ThreeFacetsPolarization-2026.pdf` — Guo, Yufan, Yilang Peng, and Tian Yang. 2026.
  "Disentangling the Three Facets of Mass Ideological Polarization: A Network Approach Across
  78 Societies." *Public Opinion Quarterly*, doi:10.1093/poq/nfag037. CC-BY. Measurement
  precedent; one magnitude score per society.
- `KileyVaisey-StabilityChangePersonalCulture-2020.pdf` — Kiley, Kevin, and Stephen Vaisey.
  2020. "Measuring Stability and Change in Personal Culture Using Panel Data." *American
  Sociological Review* 85(3):477–506, doi:10.1177/0003122420921538. OSF preprint copy. GSS
  2006–14 panels; adults mostly hold settled dispositions — the basis for cohort replacement as
  the rival to any population-level spillover.
- `Underwood-CohortSuccessionLiteraryCulture-2022.pdf` — Underwood, Ted, Kevin Kiley, Wenyi
  Shang, and Stephen Vaisey. 2022. "Cohort Succession Explains Most Change in Literary
  Culture." *Sociological Science* 9:184–205, doi:10.15195/v9.a8.

### Priority — the two the paper must argue against

- **Baldassarri, Delia, and Andrew Gelman. 2008.** "Partisans without Constraint: Political
  Polarization and Trends in American Public Opinion." *American Journal of Sociology*
  114(2):408–446. doi:10.1086/590649. *The direct predecessor* — makes the consequence claim
  for beliefs, deriving it from Blau. Paywalled.
- **Cely, Tadeas. 2025.** "One more constrained than the other: Asymmetrical ideological
  alignment and its implications for polarization." *European Journal of Political Research*
  64(4):1945–1973. doi:10.1111/1475-6765.70016. *The closest rival* — EBIC-glasso belief networks on partisan
  subpopulations. CC-BY, should be freely available.
